Subsidies and what you would actually pay

Premium tax credits are based on your household income and household size, and they are applied directly to your monthly premium rather than paid back at tax time. The sticker price of a plan on {{exchangeName}} is not what most people pay. We check what you qualify for before you pick a plan, so you are comparing what you would actually pay each month, and we tell you if a plan’s deductible or network makes the cheapest premium a poor choice.

Open enrollment in {{state}}

Open Enrollment is the annual window when anyone can sign up for a marketplace plan or change the one they have. It generally runs from November into January. {{exchangeKind}} Outside that window you need a qualifying life event — losing job coverage, moving, marriage, or a new child — to enroll. We do not publish specific dates here because they are set each plan year; a licensed agent will confirm the current ones on the call.

Turning 65 in {{state}}

If you are turning 65, or already have Medicare, a marketplace plan is usually the wrong product: once Part A and Part B begin, the premium tax credit ends and Medicare becomes primary. The natural next step is a Medicare Advantage plan for your county. See Medicare Advantage plans in {{state}}, or ask the agent on the call to walk you through the transition.
