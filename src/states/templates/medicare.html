How plan availability works in {{state}}

Medicare Advantage availability is set by county, not by state. Each plan files the counties it will serve, so two people in {{state}} can have entirely different options depending on where they live, and a plan a friend or relative has in another county may not be offered in yours. The plans open to you are the ones filed for your county, and that list is what a licensed agent compares with you.

Because of that, the only reliable way to find out what is available at your address is to ask. Call 1-888-957-3337 (TTY 711), or use the Medicare Plan Finder at medicare.gov with your ZIP code.

Free Medicare help in {{state}}

Every state runs a State Health Insurance Assistance Program: federally funded, free, and staffed by trained counselors who do not sell insurance. It is a good place to get an independent second opinion on anything a licensed agent, including ours, tells you.

When you can enroll

Initial Enrollment Period. The seven months around your 65th birthday: the three months before the month you turn 65, that month, and the three months after. If you are already receiving Social Security, Part A and Part B enrollment is usually automatic; a Medicare Advantage plan is a separate choice.

Annual Enrollment Period. Each fall, from October 15 to December 7, anyone with Medicare can join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage plan for coverage starting January 1.

Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period. From January 1 to March 31, people already in a Medicare Advantage plan can make one change: switch to another Medicare Advantage plan, or return to Original Medicare.

Special Enrollment Periods. Certain life events open a window outside those dates: moving to a new county, losing other coverage, or a change in eligibility for Medicaid or Extra Help, among others. Whether an event qualifies you is worth confirming rather than assuming; a licensed agent or {{shipName}} can tell you.

{{state}} insurance regulation

Medicare Advantage plans are approved and overseen federally by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services; the agents who sell them are licensed by the state.
