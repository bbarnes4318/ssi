Marketplace coverage

Health insurance when it doesn’t come through work

The Marketplace is where individuals and families who do not have coverage through an employer, Medicare or Medicaid buy health insurance under the Affordable Care Act. Every plan sold there covers pre-existing conditions and the same ten categories of essential benefits.

Self-employed, between jobs, retired before 65, working somewhere that does not offer insurance: this is the coverage built for those situations. A licensed agent compares the plans available in your county and checks whether you qualify for financial assistance before you choose.

Financial assistance

Your income can change what you pay

Marketplace financial assistance is based on factors including household income and household size. Two families looking at the same plan can pay very different premiums after financial assistance is applied.

That is why comparing the full cost—not just the advertised premium—is important. Not everyone qualifies, and the only way to know is to check.

Plan categories

Don’t compare health insurance by premium alone

A lower monthly premium can come with a higher deductible or higher costs when you use medical care. A higher-premium plan may make more sense for someone who expects to use healthcare regularly.

Every Marketplace plan is Bronze, Silver, Gold or Platinum. The metal says nothing about quality of care; it is the split between what you pay in premium each month and what you pay when you use care. The percentages are averages across a typical population, set by federal rule.

Required by law

The ten essential health benefits

Every Marketplace plan covers the ten essential health benefit categories required by the Affordable Care Act. What changes from plan to plan is the network, cost-sharing, covered services within those categories and other plan details.

  1. Ambulatory patient servicesOutpatient care you get without being admitted to a hospital
  2. Emergency servicesEmergency room care
  3. HospitalizationSurgery and overnight stays
  4. Pregnancy, maternity and newborn careBoth before and after birth
  5. Mental health and substance use disorder servicesIncluding behavioral health treatment
  6. Prescription drugsCovered medications, per the plan’s formulary
  7. Rehabilitative and habilitative services and devicesHelp gaining or recovering skills after an injury, disability or chronic condition
  8. Laboratory servicesTests and blood work
  9. Preventive and wellness servicesScreenings, vaccines and chronic disease management
  10. Pediatric servicesIncluding dental and vision care for children (adult dental and vision are not essential health benefits)

Plans must also cover birth control and breastfeeding support. Source: HealthCare.gov, what marketplace plans cover.

Questions

ACA health insurance questions, answered

What is ACA health insurance?

The individual and family health plans sold through the health insurance Marketplace under the Affordable Care Act. Every plan covers the ten essential health benefits, no plan can decline you or charge you more for a pre-existing condition, and financial assistance toward the premium is available to those who qualify based on household income and size.

Who can get Marketplace health insurance?

U.S. citizens and lawfully present residents who do not have affordable coverage through an employer, Medicare or Medicaid. There is no health screening. Most Marketplace buyers are under 65; people turning 65 generally move to Medicare.

How much does ACA insurance cost?

It depends on the plan tier, your county, your age, your household size and whether you qualify for financial assistance. Two households looking at the same plan can pay very different amounts after assistance. The realistic way to find out is to have your assistance checked and compare the plans in your county with it applied.

Do I qualify for a subsidy?

Premium tax credits are based on your household income and household size, and they are applied directly to your monthly premium. Not everyone qualifies. We check what you qualify for before you pick a plan, so you are comparing what you would actually pay.

Can I get ACA insurance with a pre-existing condition?

Yes. Marketplace plans cannot refuse to cover you, charge you more or exclude treatment because of a health condition you had before your coverage started.

What are Bronze, Silver, Gold and Platinum plans?

The four Marketplace plan categories. They describe how costs are split between you and the plan, not the quality of care. Bronze has the lowest premium and the highest costs when you use care; Platinum is the reverse. Silver is the only tier that carries extra cost-sharing savings for people whose income qualifies. See the tier comparison.

What happens if I lose employer health insurance?

Losing job-based coverage is a qualifying life event that opens a Special Enrollment Period, generally 60 days, to enroll in a Marketplace plan outside Open Enrollment. Call promptly; the window is short.

When can I enroll in Marketplace coverage?

During Open Enrollment, the annual window that generally runs from November into January (exact dates vary by state), or during a Special Enrollment Period opened by a qualifying life event such as losing other coverage, moving, marriage or a new child.

Can I get health insurance if I’m self-employed?

Yes. The Marketplace is built for people who do not get insurance through an employer, and self-employed people are among its most common buyers. Financial assistance is based on your household income, including self-employment income.

What happens when I turn 65?

Most people become eligible for Medicare at 65 and leave their Marketplace plan then. If you are approaching 65, a licensed agent can walk you through how Medicare Advantage compares with Original Medicare and when your enrollment window opens.
