The basics

Medicare Advantage vs. Original Medicare

One of the first decisions many Medicare beneficiaries face is how they want their Medicare coverage delivered.

Original Medicare is administered by the federal government. Medicare Advantage provides Part A and Part B coverage through a private Medicare-approved plan. You must be enrolled in both Part A and Part B to join one.

Neither choice is automatically right for everyone. Provider access, plan rules, costs and individual circumstances matter. The structural differences, side by side:

Medicare Advantage compared with Original Medicare
Original MedicareMedicare Advantage
How coverage is deliveredDirectly from the federal Medicare programThrough a private plan approved by Medicare that must cover everything Original Medicare covers
Separate partsPart A (hospital) and Part B (medical) are managed separately, each with its own rulesPart A and Part B sit under one plan, one card and one set of plan rules
Provider networkAny doctor or hospital in the U.S. that accepts MedicareEach plan sets its own network; you may need to use providers in it
Medication coverageNot included; added through a separate Medicare planUsually included in the plan; some plans leave it out
Yearly limit on what you payNone, unless you buy separate supplemental coverageEvery plan sets a yearly cap on what you pay for covered Medicare services
Who administers itThe federal government (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services)A private insurance company, under contract with Medicare

Each plan sets its own network, its own costs and its own rules. A licensed agent lays those differences side by side for the plans filed for your county.

Before you compare

What should you have ready when comparing plans?

The more information you have available, the easier it is to compare whether a plan fits the way you actually use healthcare. Nothing on this list is required to talk with us, but each item lets the agent check a plan against your real situation rather than a general one.

  1. Your Medicare cardYour Part A and Part B start dates are printed on it
  2. Your doctors’ namesEach plan has its own network, checked against its directory
  3. Your prescription listName, dose and how often, so coverage can be checked plan by plan
  4. Your current insurance informationWhatever you have now, whether Medicare, an employer plan or something else
  5. Your preferred pharmaciesPlans can treat pharmacies differently
  6. Questions about upcoming careA planned surgery, a specialist you expect to see, anything you want a plan to handle

Whether a specific doctor, medication or pharmacy is covered is verified against the plan itself before you enroll, not assumed.

Timing

When you can enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan

You can join, switch or leave a Medicare Advantage plan only during set enrollment periods. Four apply to most people.

  1. 7 months around your Medicare start

    Initial Enrollment Period

    Starts three months before you get Part A and Part B, includes the month you get them, and ends three months after. Your first chance to join a Medicare Advantage plan. Applies to everyone new to Medicare, whether at 65 or through disability.

  2. October 15 – December 7, every year

    Annual Enrollment Period

    Anyone with Medicare can join a Medicare Advantage plan, switch to a different one, or return to Original Medicare. Coverage begins January 1. This is when most people review their plan for the coming year.

  3. January 1 – March 31, every year

    Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period

    Only for people already in a Medicare Advantage plan. One change is allowed: switch to another Medicare Advantage plan, or drop the plan and return to Original Medicare. Coverage starts the first of the month after the plan receives your request.

  4. When something in your life changes

    Special Enrollment Periods

    Set windows opened by certain events: moving out of your plan’s service area, losing other coverage, qualifying for Medicaid, or qualifying for Extra Help with medication costs, among others. The window length depends on the event; a licensed agent can tell you whether one applies to you.

Enrollment period rules are set by Medicare and are published at Medicare.gov. Which periods apply to you depends on your circumstances.

Questions

Medicare Advantage questions, answered

What is Medicare Advantage?

Medicare Advantage, also called Medicare Part C, is a way to receive your Medicare Part A and Part B coverage through a private insurance company approved by Medicare, instead of directly through Original Medicare. Plans must cover everything Original Medicare covers, and each plan sets its own costs, provider network and rules.

Can I keep my doctor?

Each plan has its own provider network, and networks can change from one plan year to the next. Bring your doctors’ names; the agent checks each plan’s directory before you enroll.

How do I know if I am eligible for Medicare Advantage?

You need Part A, Part B and an address in the plan’s service area. Your Part A and Part B start dates are printed on your red, white and blue Medicare card.

What happens if I want to switch plans later?

You can usually switch Medicare Advantage plans during the Annual Enrollment Period (AEP) or other special enrollment periods, depending on your circumstances.
