Where to buy

Where North Carolina residents buy marketplace coverage

Every plan sold on the marketplace covers the ten essential health benefits, none can decline you or charge more for a pre-existing condition, and the premium tax credit is applied through the same application.

The marketplace

healthcare.gov

North Carolina marketplace plans are sold through healthcare.gov. North Carolina has no state-run exchange; the federal marketplace handles enrollment and the premium tax credit. Medicaid

Medicaid expansion in North Carolina

North Carolina has expanded Medicaid. An adult with household income up to about 138% of the federal poverty level qualifies for North Carolina Medicaid rather than a marketplace plan — in 2026 that is roughly $22,025 for one person, $29,863 for two, $37,702 for three and $45,540 for four. Above those lines the premium tax credit takes over and is applied to a marketplace plan. There is no coverage gap: the application checks income first and routes each household to the right program. Expansion status per KFF’s Medicaid expansion tracker (as of August 2026) and medicaid.gov; income lines from the 2026 HHS poverty guidelines.

Subsidies and timing

What you would actually pay, and when you can enroll

Subsidies

The sticker price is not what most people pay

Premium tax credits are based on your household income and household size, and they are applied directly to your monthly premium rather than paid back at tax time. We check what you qualify for before you pick a plan, so you are comparing what you would actually pay each month, and we tell you if a plan’s deductible or network makes the cheapest premium a poor choice.

Open enrollment

Open enrollment in North Carolina

Open Enrollment is the annual window when anyone can sign up for a marketplace plan or change the one they have. It generally runs from November into January. North Carolina uses the federal marketplace, so its window follows the healthcare.gov schedule. Outside that window you need a qualifying life event — losing job coverage, moving, marriage, or a new child — to enroll. Dates are set each plan year; a licensed agent will confirm the current ones on the call.

Turning 65

Turning 65 in North Carolina?

If you are turning 65, or already have Medicare, a marketplace plan is usually the wrong product: once Part A and Part B begin, the premium tax credit ends and Medicare becomes primary. The natural next step is a Medicare Advantage plan for your county.

FAQ

Common questions in North Carolina

Where do North Carolina residents buy ACA coverage?

Through healthcare.gov, the federal marketplace; North Carolina does not run its own exchange. The application checks North Carolina Medicaid eligibility — about $22,025 for one person in 2026 — and then calculates the premium tax credit. A licensed agent can walk through it with you at no extra cost.

Has North Carolina expanded Medicaid, and what is the income line?

Yes. Household income up to about 138% of the federal poverty level — roughly $22,025 for one person or $45,540 for a family of four in 2026 — qualifies for North Carolina Medicaid. If you were once told you earned too little for a marketplace subsidy but too much for Medicaid, that answer no longer applies in North Carolina.

What will a North Carolina marketplace plan actually cost?

The premium after your tax credit, set by household income and size and applied monthly. For a North Carolina household near the $22,025 Medicaid line for one person, the first question is whether you belong in Medicaid at all; the application settles that before any premium is quoted.

I am turning 65 in North Carolina. What changes?

Your premium tax credit ends when Medicare Part A and Part B begin, and Medicare becomes primary. North Carolina’s Seniors’ Health Insurance Information Program (SHIIP), run by the Department of Insurance at 1-855-408-1212, offers free Medicare counseling, and our agents compare the Medicare Advantage plans filed for your North Carolina county.
