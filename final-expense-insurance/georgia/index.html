Final Expense Insurance in Georgia

Direct cremation in Georgia: $2,525 median — 27% above the national average.

A direct cremation in Georgia has a median cost of $2,525 and an immediate burial $3,225 — 27% and 15% above the national medians, and the highest of the eleven states we cover. Final expense insurance covers those costs with a small whole life policy, usually $5,000 to $25,000, that pays cash directly to your family.

Georgia is the most expensive of the eleven states we cover on both measures. Cremation at $2,525 is 27% above the national median, burial at $3,225 is 15% above, and the non-declinable basic services fee of $2,578 is 17% above. Forty price lists sit behind those figures, so this is not a small-sample quirk. A Georgia family sizing a policy on national averages will come up short.

Cemetery costs are separate on every one of these figures. A plot, a vault or grave liner, the opening and closing of the grave and a headstone are billed by the cemetery, not the funeral home, and add $2,000–$5,000 or more. Regulation

Georgia insurance regulation

Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner

Final expense policies sold in Georgia are regulated by the Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner, whose Consumer Services Division describes itself as the office’s investigative arm and takes complaints about insurers and agents through its website. Ask your agent for their Georgia license number.

FAQ

Common questions

How much does a funeral cost in Georgia?

From 40 Georgia funeral home price lists collected in 2026, the median direct cremation is $2,525 and the median immediate burial $3,225 — 27% and 15% above the national medians of $1,995 and $2,800, and the highest of the eleven states we cover. Cemetery costs are separate and add $2,000–$5,000 or more.

Are funerals really more expensive in Georgia than the national average?

Yes, on every funeral-home charge we measured. The basic services fee, which cannot be declined, has a Georgia median of $2,578 against a national $2,195. With 40 price lists behind the figures, Georgia is one of the better-sampled states, so the finding is solid rather than a small-sample artifact.

How much final expense coverage does a Georgia family need?

An immediate burial at Georgia’s $3,225 median plus $2,000–$5,000 of cemetery costs is $5,225–$8,225 — the highest simplest-burial total of the eleven states we cover. A full traditional funeral with a viewing and cemetery costs comes to $8,415–$11,415 or more here. Most Georgia families should be looking at $15,000 rather than $10,000 for burial.

Are final expense premiums higher in Georgia?

No. Premiums come from the same age, gender, tobacco and health tables everywhere. What Georgia changes is the coverage amount: with cremation at $2,525 and burial at $3,225, both well above national, the same $10,000 policy covers less of a Georgia funeral than it does elsewhere, which is why $15,000 is the more common recommendation here.

Who regulates final expense insurance in Georgia?

The Georgia Office of Insurance and Safety Fire Commissioner, whose Consumer Services Division is the office’s investigative arm and takes complaints about agents and insurers through its website. Ask your agent for their Georgia license number.
