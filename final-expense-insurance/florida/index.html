Final Expense Insurance in Florida

Direct cremation in Florida: $1,882 median — 6% below the national average.

A direct cremation in Florida has a median cost of $1,882 and an immediate burial $2,695 — within a few percent of the national medians on every funeral-home charge. What moves the total for a Florida family is not the funeral home; it is the cemetery, which is left out of every published average. Final expense insurance covers both with a small whole life policy, usually $5,000 to $25,000, that pays cash directly to your family.

Florida tracks the national median closely — cremation 6% below, burial 4% below, the basic services fee 8% below — so the national averages are a fair guide to the funeral home’s bill here. The figure they leave out is the cemetery: a plot, vault, opening and closing and a marker add $2,000–$5,000 or more, and that line, not the funeral home’s, is what moves a Florida total.

Cemetery costs are separate on every one of these figures. A plot, a vault or grave liner, the opening and closing of the grave and a headstone are billed by the cemetery, not the funeral home, and add $2,000–$5,000 or more. Regulation

Florida insurance regulation

Florida Department of Financial Services, Division of Consumer Services

Final expense policies sold in Florida are regulated through the Florida Department of Financial Services, whose Division of Consumer Services runs the consumer helpline and takes complaints about agents and insurers. Unusually, the same department also regulates Florida’s funeral homes and cemeteries through its Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services — so the agency that licenses your insurance agent is also the one that oversees the funeral home’s price list.

FAQ

Common questions

How much does a funeral cost in Florida?

From 32 Florida funeral home price lists collected in 2026, the median direct cremation is $1,882 and the median immediate burial $2,695 — within a few percent of the national medians of $1,995 and $2,800. Those are funeral-home charges only; cemetery costs add $2,000–$5,000 or more on top.

Why do Florida funeral costs vary so much between quotes?

Because the cemetery is a separate business with a separate bill. Florida’s funeral-home medians are steady and close to national — $1,882 for cremation against $1,995, $2,695 for burial against $2,800 — so the spread you see between Florida quotes is mostly the cemetery, the casket and whether a viewing is included. The Florida Department of Financial Services regulates both the funeral homes and the cemeteries, and the FTC Funeral Rule entitles you to an itemized price list from any of them.

How much final expense coverage does a Florida family need?

An immediate burial at Florida’s $2,695 median plus $2,000–$5,000 of cemetery costs is $4,695–$7,695. A full traditional funeral with a viewing and cemetery costs lands at $8,415–$11,415. Add medical bills and small debts and most Florida families choose between $10,000 and $15,000.

Who do I complain to about a Florida funeral home or an insurance agent?

The same department, as it happens. The Florida Department of Financial Services licenses insurance agents through its Division of Consumer Services and regulates funeral homes and cemeteries through its Division of Funeral, Cemetery and Consumer Services. Its consumer helpline handles both; the FTC Funeral Rule also gives you the right to an itemized price list from any Florida funeral home.

Can I buy final expense insurance in Florida if I spend part of the year elsewhere?

Yes. A final expense policy is not tied to a funeral home or a state; it pays cash to the person you name, wherever the funeral takes place. If that ends up being Florida, the Florida medians — $1,882 for cremation, $2,695 for burial, plus the cemetery — are the right benchmark for the amount; if it happens in your other state, that state’s figures apply and the cash benefit is the same. Tell the agent which state is your legal residence, because a Florida-issued policy falls under the Florida Department of Financial Services’ rules.
